Units: mg (1000 = 1 g). At rest the only acceleration is gravity, so whichever axis reads +1000 is pointing UP. Flat on the table → Z ≈ +1000. Face-down → Z ≈ −1000. Standing on its edge → X or Y ≈ ±1000. Shaking it shows up as spikes on top of that. Use it for: tilt / orientation of the hand.

Gyroscope — how fast is it turning?

Units: degrees per second. Still → all zeros (no matter how it's tilted!). Each wheel spins at the speed the sensor is rotating around that axis: twist it like a doorknob around X and only the X wheel spins. It doesn't know where it is, only how fast it's turning. Use it for: motion / speed of a gesture.

Magnetometer — which way is north?

Units: µT (microtesla). It's a compass: it measures Earth's magnetic field, ~25–65 µT total. Spin the sensor flat on the table and the needle tracks north. Bring a magnet, speaker, or laptop close and the numbers jump. Use it for: absolute heading (which way the palm faces), fixing gyro drift.

Movement — which way did the hand just move?

The accelerometer reads gravity + movement mixed together. This panel tracks gravity with a slow filter and subtracts it, leaving just your push: linear acceleration in mg. The ball is nudged by that push and springs back to center; the ball grows for forward (+Y) and shrinks for back. The arrow shows the direction of the start of a move (the stop at the end pushes the opposite way — that's real physics, not a bug). Note: tilting the sensor shows up briefly as movement too, since the filter takes a moment to catch up. Directions assume +X = right, +Y = forward, +Z = up on the glove; tell me if yours is mounted differently.
