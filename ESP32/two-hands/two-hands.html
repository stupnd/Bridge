Move one hand at a time and watch only that hand's lines react — that's the two sensors working independently on the same two wires. These are all orientation and motion measurements: which way each palm faces and how fast it's turning. An IMU cannot tell you where a hand is — position needs double-integrating acceleration, which drifts to meters of error within seconds. For ASL that's usually fine: palm orientation, handshape and movement carry most of the signal. Body-relative location would need a camera, or more IMUs up the arm so limb angles give position by geometry.
